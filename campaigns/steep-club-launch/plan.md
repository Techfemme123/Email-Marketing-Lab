# The Steep Club Launch: Campaign Plan

Draft plan. Nothing here has been sent. All emails are drafts until you approve them.

## Assumptions to check

- The waitlist is about 2,000 people. I don't know how big each of the three groups is, so sizes are marked "to confirm".

## 1. Campaign overview

- **Name:** "Open the Magical Box" (working name; the campaign itself is the launch of The Steep Club)
- **Summary:** Over four weeks, email our waitlist friends about The Steep Club, the monthly tea box with a surprise inside, and invite them to start a subscription.
- **Primary goal:** 300 waitlist members start a subscription between 7 Dec and 3 Jan. That is 15% of about 2,000 people.
- **Secondary goals:**
  - Keep unsubscribes under 1% of the list across the campaign.
  - Learn which of the three groups subscribes most, so we know who to write to next.
  - Collect a few kind words from early members to use later.

**A note on the goal.** 15% is a bold target for a waitlist, though these people asked to hear from us, so it is not unreasonable. Treat the weekly checkpoints in section 7 as a guide, not a verdict. If we are well behind after week 1, we adjust the emails, not the target.

## 2. Audience

All 2,000 people chose to join our waitlist. They are aged 40 to 90: busy professionals, students and retired people. They want good flavour, especially ilaichi, and an aroma that greets them before the first sip.

| Group | Who they are | What they care about | What might hold them back | Size |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tea-curious beginners | New to loose-leaf or specialty tea | Easy start, no wrong answers, a gentle way to explore | Worry it's too fancy or they'll choose wrong | to confirm |
| Gift buyers | Want to give tea to someone | A thoughtful gift that feels personal | Unsure it will arrive on time; unsure if it suits the person | to confirm |
| Daily tea drinkers | Already drink tea every day | Better flavour, new finds, a monthly treat | They already have a favourite; why switch or add? | to confirm |

**Where they are:** their inbox. This campaign is email only, so every message has to work on a phone and be easy to read (large text, one clear button).

**Buying stage:** consideration to decision. They already raised their hand. They need a reason, reassurance and an easy next step.

## 3. Key messages

**Core message:** Your waitlist spot is now a seat at the table. The Steep Club brings a fresh, fragrant surprise to your door every month.

**Supporting messages:**

1. **A magical box and a surprise every month.** Something new to look forward to. *Proof:* a clear list or photo of what is in the first box.
2. **An aroma that greets you before the first sip.** Fresh, fragrant tea, with ilaichi as a thread through the club. *Proof:* real photos, and honest tasting notes from the team.
3. **Easy and low-risk.** Simple to start, easy to pause or cancel. *Proof:* a plain-English FAQ. Only say this if it is true.
4. **A gift that keeps arriving** (gift buyers). One gift, twelve small moments. *Proof:* gift option details, once decided.

**Tone by group** (same Kettlebird voice: charming, warm, chatty):

- Beginners: reassuring, "no tea knowledge needed".
- Gift buyers: thoughtful, about the person receiving it.
- Daily drinkers: curious, about the aroma and the surprise.

**Brand rules that apply to every email** (from `brand/voice.md`):

- Call readers "friends". No "Dear customer".
- No pressure words: "hurry", "limited time", "buy now". No ALL CAPS, no "!!!", no slang.
- One emoji per email, at most.
- No health or weight-loss claims. No "best", "finest" or "No. 1". No "organic" or "100% natural" without proof.
- No exact delivery date promise. No "prices will never change".
- Name a tea's origin (for example Ceylon) only if the supplier can prove it.
- Close with "Until your next cup", sign as "Ara, from the Kettlebird team", and end with a **P.S.** brewing tip.

## 4. Channel strategy

**Email only.** No paid ads, as requested.

- **Why it fits:** the audience is warm and already on our list. Email is where they expect to hear from us, and it costs very little.
- **Formats:** short, friendly emails with one photo and one button. Plain-text feel is fine and often warmer.
- **Effort:** low to medium. About 9 emails, with 3 versions for the group-specific ones.
- **Segmentation:** tag every waitlist member as beginner, gift buyer or daily drinker. If we don't already know this, a one-question "what brings you to tea?" click in the first email can sort people.
- **Landing page:** one simple Steep Club page (what's in the box, price, FAQ, sign-up button). This is the only non-email asset and the whole campaign points to it.

## 7. Success metrics

**Primary KPI:** 300 new subscriptions from the waitlist by 3 Jan.

**Weekly checkpoints** (a guide, with most sign-ups expected early):

| By end of | Cumulative subscriptions |
|---|---|
| Week 1 (13 Dec) | 100 |
| Week 2 (20 Dec) | 190 |
| Week 3 (27 Dec) | 250 |
| Week 4 (3 Jan) | 300 |

**Secondary KPIs:**

- Open rate per email (baseline for a warm waitlist is usually healthy; we'll set our own baseline from email 1).
- Click rate on the sign-up button.
- Landing page to subscription conversion.
- Subscriptions by group (beginner, gift, daily).
- Unsubscribe and spam complaint rates (watch for any jump).

**Tracking:** the email tool for opens and clicks, and the subscription or checkout system for sign-ups. Use a tagged link per email so we can tell which email drove each sign-up.

**Reporting:** a short check every Monday (7 days of numbers), and a wrap-up on 5 Jan.

## 9. Risks and what we do about them

1. **15% sign-up is too optimistic.** *What we do:* check at each weekly checkpoint, improve the subject lines and landing page, and use the flexible slots to resend to non-openers. Re-set the expectation after week 2 if needed.
2. **Christmas week lowers attention, and gift buyers worry about delivery.** *What we do:* send fewer emails that week, pause 24 to 27 Dec, and never promise a delivery date. Be clear about what we can say, for example "your first box ships after you join" only if it is true.
3. **Too many emails tire people out.** *What we do:* stop sales emails the moment someone subscribes, send only to non-subscribers after week 1, and watch unsubscribes. If they pass 1%, cut email 8 or 9.
4. **We say something we can't prove** (origin, "natural", health). *What we do:* check every draft against `brand/voice.md` before approval, using the brand review step.
